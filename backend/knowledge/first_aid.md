## First Aid Essentials

### Minor Burns (First-Degree Burns)
First-degree burns affect only the outer layer of skin, causing redness, minor swelling, and pain.
- **Immediate Action**: Cool the burn immediately with cool running tap water for 10 to 15 minutes. Do NOT use ice, butter, or oil on burns.
- **Protection**: Cover gently with a clean, non-stick sterile bandage.
- **Pain Relief**: Over-the-counter pain relievers can help reduce swelling and discomfort.
- **Medical Attention**: Seek medical care if the burn covers a large area, involves face, hands, feet, or major joints, or shows signs of infection (pus, increasing redness).

### Minor Cuts & Scrapes
- **Cleanliness**: Wash your hands before touching the wound.
- **Stop Bleeding**: Apply gentle, steady pressure with a clean cloth or bandage.
- **Clean the Wound**: Rinse with clean water. Clean around the wound with mild soap; do not apply harsh chemicals like iodine or hydrogen peroxide directly into deep tissue.
- **Protect**: Apply petroleum jelly or an antibiotic ointment, and cover with a sterile bandage.

### Choking (Conscious Adult First Aid)
- **Signs of Severe Choking**: Inability to speak, cough, or breathe; clutching the throat (universal choking sign).
- **Abdominal Thrusts (Heimlich Maneuver)**:
  1. Stand behind the person and wrap your arms around their waist.
  2. Make a fist with one hand and place it just above the person's navel.
  3. Grasp your fist with your other hand and press hard into the abdomen with a quick, upward thrust.
  4. Repeat until the object is dislodged. Call emergency services immediately if the person loses consciousness.
