## General Diseases Overview

### Hypertension (High Blood Pressure)
Hypertension is a common condition in which the long-term force of the blood against your artery walls is high enough that it may eventually cause health problems, such as heart disease.
- **Definition**: Blood pressure reading consistently above 130/80 mmHg.
- **Symptoms**: Often called a "silent killer" because it typically has no visible symptoms until significant damage occurs.
- **Management & Prevention**: Reducing sodium intake, exercising regularly (150 minutes per week), maintaining a healthy weight, limiting alcohol, avoiding tobacco, and taking prescribed antihypertensive medications as advised by a physician.

### Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus
Type 2 diabetes is a chronic condition that affects the way your body processes blood sugar (glucose).
- **Causes**: The body develops insulin resistance or does not produce enough insulin to maintain normal glucose levels.
- **Common Symptoms**: Increased thirst, frequent urination, unexplained weight loss, fatigue, blurry vision, and slow-healing sores.
- **Management**: Regular blood sugar monitoring, balanced diet rich in fiber and lean proteins, regular physical activity, and oral medications or insulin injections prescribed by a doctor.

### Asthma
Asthma is a condition in which your airways narrow and swell and may produce extra mucus.
- **Symptoms**: Shortness of breath, chest tightness or pain, wheezing when exhaling, and coughing fits.
- **Triggers**: Airborne allergens (pollen, dust mites), cold air, physical exertion, respiratory infections, and stress.
- **Care Plan**: Identifying and avoiding triggers, using long-term control medications (inhaled corticosteroids) and quick-relief rescue inhalers (bronchodilators) under medical supervision.

### Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD)
GERD occurs when stomach acid repeatedly flows back into the tube connecting your mouth and stomach (esophagus).
- **Symptoms**: Heartburn (burning sensation in chest), regurgitation of food or sour liquid, difficulty swallowing, and chest pain.
- **Lifestyle Adjustments**: Eating smaller meals, avoiding trigger foods (fatty, spicy, caffeine, chocolate), staying upright for 3 hours after eating, and elevating the head of the bed.
